💥1 Overview
Four-rotor drones are characterized by their vertical takeoff and landing, free hovering, and agile flight capabilities. They have been applied in various fields such as high-altitude aerial photography, resource exploration, and emergency rescue operations, showing promising prospects. However, due to their multi-input, underactuated, and strong coupling characteristics, designing a highly accurate attitude control system with anti-interference capabilities in complex environments has always been a challenging theoretical problem.
Elkhatem et al. [1] proposed a robust linear quadratic regulator (LQR) controller and an LQR-proportional integral (PID) controller for the position and attitude tracking of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs). Both control strategies can stabilize the four-rotor UAVs in desired positions and attitudes, offering superior performance and robustness. Yang Xu et al. [4] introduced a self-coupled proportional-integral-derivative (PID) controller for the attitude control of quadrotor UAVs. The effectiveness of the proposed algorithm was verified through simulation experiments in Matlab/Simulink, with the best case of attitude control overshooting controlled to around 6%. Phinguyen et al. [3] addressed the sliding mode control issue under external disturbances by proposing an adaptive sliding mode controller based on neural networks for UAV attitude control. The proposed control method demonstrated good performance, strong disturbance rejection capabilities, and tracking performance.
